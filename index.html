Persian Gulf

U.S. and Iran trade the heaviest fire since July as Hormuz shipping stays contested

UPDATED · Wed. 8:30 a.m. EDT

Overnight into Wednesday, U.S. forces struck Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps air-defense, radar, maritime, and communications sites along Iran’s southern coast, and Iran fired missiles and drones at American facilities in Jordan, Bahrain, Kuwait, and northern Iraq, according to CENTCOM and regional militaries. Iranian officials said a U.S. strike hit a house hosting a wedding. Iran’s Revolutionary Guards said two tankers struck naval mines in the Strait of Hormuz. President Trump said he is not seeking a new agreement with Tehran and warned of harder strikes if Iran retaliates. Jordan and Bahrain reported intercepts; the U.S. said it had no casualties from Iranian fire.

Supreme Court

Supreme Court, 5–4, allows White House ballroom construction to continue

An unsigned order pauses a federal judge’s halt on above-ground work. The majority said the National Trust for Historic Preservation likely lacks standing and did not decide whether Congress had to approve demolition of the East Wing. The administration has told the court the project is about two-thirds complete and includes underground security construction.

Center-left view

Chief Justice John Roberts joined Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson. Roberts wrote that “the White House is not just any building,” that construction is “likely unlawful,” and that the result is “no victory for the separation of powers.” Lower courts had said the work likely required a statute Congress did not pass. POLITICO, SCOTUSblog, and Slate report the preservation challenge that way.

Sources: POLITICO · SCOTUSblog · Slate

Center-right view

The majority said the Trust had not shown a concrete injury and credited the government’s filing that stopping a 250-person, 20-hour-a-day job would create security and engineering risk. Underground bunkers were already allowed to proceed. Solicitor General D. John Sauer’s briefs, as summarized by SCOTUSblog and JURIST, treat historic-preservation standing as the issue, not a public veto on the working residence.

Sources: Court majority via SCOTUSblog · JURIST · Solicitor General D. John Sauer filings, quoted in those reports

Defense Department

Army Secretary Dan Driscoll submits resignation after 18 months

Administration officials said Driscoll handed President Trump his resignation on Monday. ABC News reports he had repeatedly warned the White House and chief of staff Susie Wiles about Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth’s firings of senior officers. He had been Army secretary for 18 months. The Army has had no Senate-confirmed chief of staff since April and now also lacks a confirmed secretary amid the Iran war.

Center-left view

The New York Times, The Atlantic, and ABC say Driscoll told the president Hegseth’s removal of senior officers and blocked promotions is stalling Army modernization while the U.S. is in a live Iran fight and still supporting Ukraine. Those accounts say the largest service is without a confirmed secretary, chief of staff, or Europe commander at the same time.

Sources: New York Times · The Atlantic · ABC / Pentagon pool report

Center-right view

Hegseth has told House appropriators that a department “destroyed by the wrong perspectives” cannot be changed with the same officers who ran it. The White House statement did not describe Driscoll’s exit as a rebuke of the secretary. POLITICO and the BBC quote officials who cast the personnel moves as execution of the president’s agenda in wartime.

Sources: White House statement via BBC · POLITICO · Hegseth testimony to House appropriators, quoted in the Pentagon pool report

Mail-ballot processing · file

Postal Service

Senator releases USPS whistleblower complaint; hearing set for Sept. 3

Sen. Richard Blumenthal, D-Conn., made public a complaint from an anonymous official represented by Whistleblower Aid. The complaint says the Postal Service built an online portal to carry out President Trump’s executive order on mail voting and planned a September launch. It describes a rule under which a failed barcode scan could return an entire batch of ballots. A Senate Homeland Security subcommittee hearing on the disclosure is set for Thursday, Sept. 3. The underlying order remains in court.

Center-left view

Blumenthal calls the project “rushed, chaotic and fundamentally flawed” and says it risks keeping lawful mail ballots from voters before the midterms. The Guardian and elections desks quote voting-rights groups who call the executive order a federal insertion into a state function and say large-scale mail-ballot fraud has not been shown in post-election audits.

Sources: Sen. Blumenthal · The Guardian · Jane C. Timm, elections report

Center-right view

A center-right political desk reports that after a Supreme Court order allowing planning to continue, agencies can implement citizenship lists, unique ballot barcodes, and Postal Service rules on who receives election mail. Former White House deputy counsel Gene Hamilton called the order an election-integrity measure. The administration’s public case is chain of custody, not a change to who is eligible to vote in person.

Sources: Charles Creitz, Aug. 28 political report · Supreme Court stay, via New York Times · March 2026 executive order, as described in those reports

Congress

House votes 370–48 to fund the government through Dec. 11; bill awaits signature

The continuing resolution, already passed by the Senate, holds most agency budgets at current levels through Dec. 11. The text delays a scheduled ban on intoxicating hemp products, blocks a White House rule that would put political appointees over federal-grant approval, and bars a transfer of funds to Border Patrol. It is on President Trump’s desk and has not been signed as of Wednesday morning public wires; officials expect a signature before the Sept. 30 fiscal-year end.

Center-left view

The New York Times, The Washington Post, and Roll Call describe the grant-review freeze and the Border Patrol funding lock as conditions Democrats required for votes. Those reports present the 370–48 tally as both parties choosing to avoid a funding lapse in the weeks before the midterms.

Sources: New York Times · Washington Post · Roll Call

Center-right view

Speaker Mike Johnson and Appropriations Chair Tom Cole call the bill a short-term patch that keeps agencies open at current levels through the election. POLITICO and the Daily Caller report that some House Republicans voted no over the hemp delay, the absence of added immigration funding, and the lack of spending cuts in a Senate draft the president had endorsed.

Sources: POLITICO · Rep. Tom Cole via Government Executive · Daily Caller

Arizona

Park Service identifies two dead in Grand Canyon flood; one hiker still missing

The National Park Service identified John Giusti, 46, and Brent Rosenkranz, 42, both of Granbury, Texas, as the two people found dead after Saturday’s flash flood along Bright Angel Creek. Timothy Allen Smith, 53, remains missing. Rangers and helicopters evacuated 82 people from the Phantom Ranch area. The search continues with dogs, boats, and helicopters.

Center-left view

NBC News, CNN, and other national desks place the flood in a summer of sudden Western monsoon runoff. They note that a creek gauge triggered an alert and that the volume still wrecked a historic inner-canyon corridor. Climate desks in that coverage point to warmer air holding more moisture; they treat monsoon bursts as familiar and the damage scale as the news.

Sources: NBC News · CNN · Reuters via The Star

Center-right view

AZFamily, the Associated Press, and National Park Service briefings focus on the identification of the two victims, the continuing search for Smith, and an operational airlift that accounted for all but one known person in the zone. County and park briefings describe a monsoon debris flow in a slot canyon — a known seasonal hazard — and a search now concentrated on the river corridor.

Sources: AZFamily / NPS · Associated Press · National Park Service briefings, quoted in those reports

Gulf Coast

Tropical Depression Edouard triggers East Texas flash flood emergency

The National Weather Service issued a Flash Flood Emergency for Hardin and Tyler counties early Wednesday as the remnants of Edouard pushed inland. National Hurricane Center guidance calls for rainfall totals up to about 12 inches in parts of East Texas; radar estimates in some counties ran higher. Flood watches extend into Wednesday evening. Fox Weather reported more than 70,000 customers without power. Port Arthur recorded gusts near 90 mph at landfall Tuesday. The center was near the Lufkin area Wednesday morning and was forecast to weaken and dissipate Wednesday night.

Center-left view

CNN and the Weather Channel note that warm Gulf water helped a depression become a named storm within a day. That coverage centers on inland flood risk from the Upper Texas coast into east-central Texas, not on wind damage at the coast, and treats rapid intensification over a warm Gulf as the climate context for the rain threat.

Sources: CNN · Weather Channel · National Hurricane Center

Center-right view

Fox Weather and Gov. Greg Abbott’s office frame the story as an operational response: a disaster declaration covering Hardin, Jasper, Jefferson, Newton, Panola, Shelby, and Tyler counties, power restoration, and National Hurricane Center track guidance as the depression weakens inland.

Sources: Fox Weather · Gov. Abbott disaster declaration · National Hurricane Center advisories, quoted in those reports
