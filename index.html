Miami

NTSB recovers flight recorders after Miami Amazon cargo overrun; 5 dead on ground

UPDATED · Tue. morning EDT

Amazon Air Flight 7598, a Boeing 767 operated by 21 Air, overran a Miami International Airport runway around 1:53 p.m. Sunday after arriving from San Juan, Puerto Rico. The jet struck a cleaning-crew van with seven people aboard inside the airport fence and a Toyota outside; all five people killed were in the vehicles, and the flight crew survived. At a Monday briefing, NTSB Chair Jennifer Homendy said the flight data and cockpit voice recorders had been recovered and sent to NTSB headquarters in Washington, with 32 investigators on a “devastating” scene still in fact-finding. Two of four runways remained closed, fuel removal was ongoing, and the crew had not yet been interviewed.

Midterms

U.S. Supreme Court weighs Missouri map stay as Sept. 8 ballot deadline hits

Missouri Secretary of State Denny Hoskins filed emergency stay application 26A304 with Justice Brett Kavanaugh seeking to pause the state Supreme Court’s order that keeps the 2022 congressional map for November and places a referendum on HB 1. Responses were due noon Sept. 7; as of Tuesday morning no stay order had issued. State officials say Sept. 8 is the ballot-alteration deadline and UOCAVA ballots are due Sept. 19. Challengers including von Glahn and the ACLU urge denial. The 2022 map remains ordered for the Nov. 3 midterms unless the U.S. Supreme Court stays the state ruling.

DOJ renews Supreme Court ask after judge blocks USPS mail-ballot rule

U.S. District Judge Indira Talwani issued a Sept. 4 preliminary injunction blocking mandatory parts of the Postal Service’s Aug. 26 Ballot Mail for Federal Elections rule for the Nov. 3 midterms, replacing her earlier temporary restraining order. On Sunday the Justice Department withdrew its Sept. 3 Supreme Court application aimed at the TRO and filed a new emergency stay application (26A305) against the preliminary injunction, with Solicitor General D. Trade

Canada’s retaliatory tariffs on U.S. goods take effect as talks stall

Canada’s counter-tariffs took effect at 12:01 a.m. ET Tuesday on roughly $20–27.6 billion of U.S. goods at rates of 15–50%, matching U.S. levies dollar-for-dollar after August talks collapsed. The list covers steel, appliances, dairy, agricultural equipment, electronics, and other categories. Prime Minister Mark Carney framed the move as protection for Canadian workers; President Trump had imposed 50% tariffs and used “51st state” rhetoric. Fresh fish and lobster were later omitted after industry pushback, per BBC reporting.

Russia resumes Kyiv strikes after U.S. envoys leave; capital pause ends

Persian Gulf
