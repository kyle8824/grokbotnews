Midterms

Missouri Supreme Court blocks Trump-backed House map for November

The Missouri Supreme Court unanimously ruled Thursday that the 2025 congressional map (HB 1) cannot be used in the Nov. 3 midterms unless voters first approve it in a referendum, and ordered Secretary of State Denny Hoskins to place the referendum on the ballot by Sept. 8. The court said the 2022 districts remain in force because a timely petition with more than 300,000 signatures suspended HB 1. Attorney General Catherine Hanaway said she would appeal to the U.S. Supreme Court; President Trump called the ruling “horrible, ridiculous, and unConstitutional.” The August primary used the 2025 lines; the court did not undo those primary results.

Center-left view

NBC News, Al Jazeera, the Missouri Independent, and Democracy Docket frame a unanimous state high-court block on a Trump-backed House map, a voter-driven referendum ordered onto the November ballot, and the return to 2022 districts for the general election.

Sources: NBC News · Al Jazeera · Missouri Independent · Democracy Docket

Center-right view

Fox News, NOTUS, and The Missouri Times frame the state high court’s block on GOP-backed maps ahead of the midterms, Trump’s rebuke of the ruling, and the path to a November referendum vote on the map.

Sources: Fox News · NOTUS · The Missouri Times

Postal Service

DOJ asks Supreme Court to clear USPS mail-ballot rule before midterms

The Justice Department on Thursday asked the Supreme Court to stay U.S. District Judge Indira Talwani’s order blocking mandatory parts of the Postal Service’s Aug. 26 Ballot Mail for Federal Elections rule, seeking an administrative stay while states begin mailing. Solicitor General D. John Sauer argued North Carolina and Alabama start sending ballots as soon as Sept. 4 and that envelopes cannot be retrieved once mailed. Justice Jackson directed respondents to oppose by Sept. 8. Talwani held a hearing Thursday on extending the TRO; the portal for voter/barcode data is not yet operational.

Center-left view

NBC News, Reuters, SCOTUSblog, and CNN frame DOJ’s emergency Supreme Court ask against Talwani’s block, Purcell and midterm-mailing timing, and the still-dark ballot-mail portal as states prepare to send envelopes.

Sources: NBC News · Reuters · SCOTUSblog · CNN

Congress

Trump signs stopgap funding bill through Dec. 11

President Trump on Wednesday signed the Continuing Appropriations and Extensions Act, 2027, funding federal agencies through Dec. 11 and averting a shutdown threat before the fiscal year ends Sept. 30. The measure passed the House 370–48 and the Senate 90–6. It delays a scheduled ban on intoxicating hemp products, blocks an OMB grant-review rule, and includes other riders. Nineteen House Republicans voted no.

Center-left view

The Hill and Washington Post coverage present the bipartisan vote as averting a shutdown ahead of the midterms; Democrats secured grant-rule and Border Patrol transfer conditions in the text.

Sources: The Hill · Congress.gov (H.R. 6500) · Washington Post

Center-right view

Epoch Times, POLITICO, and the Daily Caller frame a Johnson/Cole short-term patch through the election; some GOP no votes cited the hemp delay and the lack of spending cuts or immigration add-ons.

Sources: Epoch Times · POLITICO · Daily Caller

Supreme Court

Supreme Court, 5–4, allows White House ballroom construction to continue

An unsigned order pauses a federal judge’s halt on above-ground work. The majority said the National Trust for Historic Preservation likely lacks standing and did not decide whether Congress had to approve demolition of the East Wing. The administration has told the court the project is about two-thirds complete and includes underground security construction.

Center-left view

Chief Justice John Roberts joined Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson. Roberts wrote that “the White House is not just any building,” that construction is “likely unlawful,” and that the result is “no victory for the separation of powers.” Lower courts had said the work likely required a statute Congress did not pass. POLITICO, SCOTUSblog, and Slate report the preservation challenge that way.

Sources: POLITICO · SCOTUSblog · Slate

Center-right view

The majority said the Trust had not shown a concrete injury and credited the government’s filing that stopping a 250-person, 20-hour-a-day job would create security and engineering risk. Underground bunkers were already allowed to proceed. Solicitor General D. John Sauer’s briefs, as summarized by SCOTUSblog and JURIST, treat historic-preservation standing as the issue, not a public veto on the working residence.

Sources: Court majority via SCOTUSblog · JURIST · Solicitor General D. John Sauer filings, quoted in those reports
