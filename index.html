Massachusetts

Judge declares mistrial in Lindsay Clancy triple-murder case after hung jury

NEW · Tue. midday EDT

Judge William Sullivan declared a mistrial Sept. 4 in Plymouth Superior Court after jurors reported they remained deadlocked following about seven days of deliberations in the murder trial of Lindsay Clancy, charged in the 2023 deaths of her children Cora, Dawson, and Callan. Reporting described an 11-1 split; the defense argued postpartum psychosis, and prosecutors said she knew right from wrong. Plymouth County District Attorney Tim Cruz has not decided whether to retry; a status conference is set for Sept. 29. Clancy remains held at Tewksbury State Hospital. Patrick Clancy, through counsel, called the prospect of another trial “extraordinarily painful.”

Miami

NTSB recovers flight recorders after Miami Amazon cargo overrun; 5 dead on ground

Amazon Air Flight 7598, a Boeing 767 operated by 21 Air, overran a Miami International Airport runway around 1:53 p.m. Sunday after arriving from San Juan, Puerto Rico. The jet struck a cleaning-crew van with seven people aboard inside the airport fence and a Toyota outside; all five people killed were in the vehicles, and the flight crew survived. At a Monday briefing, NTSB Chair Jennifer Homendy said the flight data and cockpit voice recorders had been recovered and sent to NTSB headquarters in Washington, with 32 investigators on a “devastating” scene still in fact-finding. Two of four runways remained closed, fuel removal was ongoing, and the crew had not yet been interviewed.

BBC, Guardian, and CBS Miami stress the ground death toll, hub delays with runways still closed, and victim recovery before full wreckage access after Homendy’s Monday briefing.

Sources: BBC · Guardian · CBS Miami

Center-right view

UPI, Fox 8/AP, and FreightWaves emphasize NTSB process, black boxes en route to Washington, EMAS/arresting questions, operator 21 Air, and airport capacity with two runways still closed.

Ohio

Armed man arrested after pushing toward Ohio gov candidate Amy Acton at Canfield Fair

Patrick Havas, 38, of Canfield was arrested Sunday inside a Democratic Party tent at the Canfield Fair after pushing through a crowd while gubernatorial candidate Amy Acton was speaking, according to the Mahoning County Sheriff’s Office and Ohio State Highway Patrol. Two people were knocked down; Acton was not injured. Deputies recovered two handguns and brass knuckles; investigators said no weapons were brandished or removed from holsters. Havas was booked on assault and disorderly conduct counts. Gov. Mike DeWine called violence or its threat at political events unacceptable.

Center-left view

PBS NewsHour/AP and ABC6 emphasize an armed man charging through a crowd toward Acton, injuries to bystanders, and Highway Patrol troopers on her protective detail making the stop.

Sources: PBS NewsHour / AP · ABC6 / WSYX · AP via PBS (DeWine)

Center-right view

Sheriff and local desks stress that weapons were recovered but never brandished, Havas was filming as he entered, and the case remains under investigation with assault and disorderly counts filed.

Homeland Security

DHS secretary says ICE will not patrol polls but could enter for threats or warrants

Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin told reporters in New York that ICE “isn’t there to patrol the polling places” but could be present if there is a threat to a site or to serve a warrant on someone agents are actively tracking. A senior DHS official told NBC News that ICE officers have not received guidance to do anything beyond regular duties on Election Day. Democrats and voting-rights groups warn that federal agents at polls could intimidate voters; Mullin and DHS say any presence would be limited to specific threats or warrant service, not election patrols.

Midterms

U.S. Supreme Court weighs Missouri map stay as Sept. 8 ballot deadline hits

Missouri Secretary of State Denny Hoskins filed emergency stay application 26A304 with Justice Brett Kavanaugh seeking to pause the state Supreme Court’s order that keeps the 2022 congressional map for November and places a referendum on HB 1. Responses were due noon Sept. 7; as of Tuesday midday no stay order had issued. State officials say Sept. 8 is the ballot-alteration deadline and UOCAVA ballots are due Sept. 19. Challengers including von Glahn and the ACLU urge denial. The 2022 map remains ordered for the Nov. 3 midterms unless the U.S. Supreme Court stays the state ruling.

Congress

Trump signs stopgap funding bill through Dec. 11

President Trump on Wednesday signed the Continuing Appropriations and Extensions Act, 2027, funding federal agencies through Dec. 11 and averting a shutdown threat before the fiscal year ends Sept. 30. The measure passed the House 370–48 and the Senate 90–6. It delays a scheduled ban on intoxicating hemp products, blocks an OMB grant-review rule, and includes other riders. Nineteen House Republicans voted no.

Center-left view

The Hill and Washington Post coverage present the bipartisan vote as averting a shutdown ahead of the midterms; Democrats secured grant-rule and Border Patrol transfer conditions in the text.

Trade

Canada’s retaliatory tariffs on U.S. goods take effect as talks stall

Canada’s counter-tariffs took effect at 12:01 a.m. ET Tuesday on roughly $20–28 billion of U.S. goods at rates of 15–50%, matching U.S. levies dollar-for-dollar after August talks collapsed. The list covers steel, appliances, dairy, agricultural equipment, electronics, and other categories. Prime Minister Mark Carney framed the move as protection for Canadian workers; President Trump had imposed 50% tariffs and used “51st state” rhetoric. Fresh fish and lobster were later omitted after industry pushback, per BBC reporting.

Iran plans Hormuz exclusion zone as oil holds near $97; U.S. denies ship-strike claim

Bahamas

Coast Guard joins Bahamas search for U.S. family’s missing plane near Andros

A PA-34 Piper five-seater (N212ML) carrying four South Florida family members lost contact with air traffic control about 13 miles west of Andros Island on Monday after departing Great Harbour Cay for a Miami-area airport. Bahamian authorities requested U.S. Coast Guard help; an HC-144 Ocean Sentry was launched. The family identified the occupants as Mario Lamar Sr., 80; Lili Lamar, 79; and grandchildren Sofi and Christian Sosa. Poor weather suspended the search for a time; agencies said efforts would resume when conditions allow.
