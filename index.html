Supreme Court

Supreme Court, 5–4, allows White House ballroom construction to continue

An unsigned order pauses a federal judge’s halt on above-ground work. The majority said the National Trust for Historic Preservation likely lacks standing and did not decide whether Congress had to approve demolition of the East Wing. The administration has told the court the project is about two-thirds complete and includes underground security construction.

Center-left view

Chief Justice John Roberts joined Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson. Roberts wrote that “the White House is not just any building,” that construction is “likely unlawful,” and that the result is “no victory for the separation of powers.” Lower courts had said the work likely required a statute Congress did not pass. POLITICO, SCOTUSblog, and Slate report the preservation challenge that way.

Sources: POLITICO · SCOTUSblog · Slate

Center-right view

The majority said the Trust had not shown a concrete injury and credited the government’s filing that stopping a 250-person, 20-hour-a-day job would create security and engineering risk. Underground bunkers were already allowed to proceed. Solicitor General D. John Sauer’s briefs, as summarized by SCOTUSblog and JURIST, treat historic-preservation standing as the issue, not a public veto on the working residence.

Sources: Court majority via SCOTUSblog · JURIST · Solicitor General D. John Sauer filings, quoted in those reports

The Pentagon · file

Defense Department

Army Secretary Dan Driscoll submits resignation after 18 months

Administration officials said Driscoll handed President Trump his resignation on Monday. He had been Army secretary for 18 months. The Army has had no Senate-confirmed chief of staff since April.

Center-left view

The New York Times, The Atlantic, and a Tuesday Pentagon pool report say Driscoll told the president Hegseth’s removal of senior officers and blocked promotions is stalling Army modernization while the U.S. is in a live Iran fight and still supporting Ukraine. Those accounts say the largest service is without a confirmed secretary, chief of staff, or Europe commander at the same time.

Sources: New York Times · The Atlantic · Pentagon pool report

Center-right view

Hegseth has told House appropriators that a department “destroyed by the wrong perspectives” cannot be changed with the same officers who ran it. The White House statement did not describe Driscoll’s exit as a rebuke of the secretary. POLITICO and the BBC quote officials who cast the personnel moves as execution of the president’s agenda in wartime.

Sources: White House statement via BBC · POLITICO · Hegseth testimony to House appropriators, quoted in the Pentagon pool report

Mail-ballot processing · file

Postal Service

Senator releases USPS whistleblower complaint on mail-ballot screening

Sen. Richard Blumenthal, D-Conn., made public a complaint from an anonymous official represented by Whistleblower Aid. The complaint says the Postal Service built an online portal to carry out President Trump’s executive order on mail voting and planned a September launch. It describes a rule under which a failed barcode scan could return an entire batch of ballots. The underlying order remains in court.

Center-left view

Blumenthal calls the project “rushed, chaotic and fundamentally flawed” and says it risks keeping lawful mail ballots from voters before the midterms. The Guardian and a Tuesday elections desk quote voting-rights groups who call the executive order a federal insertion into a state function and say large-scale mail-ballot fraud has not been shown in post-election audits.

Sources: Sen. Blumenthal · The Guardian · Jane C. Timm, elections report

Center-right view

A center-right political desk reports that after a Supreme Court order allowing planning to continue, agencies can implement citizenship lists, unique ballot barcodes, and Postal Service rules on who receives election mail. Former White House deputy counsel Gene Hamilton called the order an election-integrity measure. The administration’s public case is chain of custody, not a change to who is eligible to vote in person.

Sources: Charles Creitz, Aug. 28 political report · Supreme Court stay, via New York Times · March 2026 executive order, as described in those reports

U.S. Capitol

Congress

House votes 370–48 to fund the government through Dec. 11

The continuing resolution, already passed by the Senate, holds most agency budgets at current levels through Dec. 11. The text delays a scheduled ban on intoxicating hemp products, blocks a White House rule that would put political appointees over federal-grant approval, and bars a transfer of funds to Border Patrol. It now goes to President Trump.

Center-left view

The New York Times, The Washington Post, and Roll Call describe the grant-review freeze and the Border Patrol funding lock as conditions Democrats required for votes. Those reports present the 370–48 tally as both parties choosing to avoid a funding lapse in the weeks before the midterms.

Sources: New York Times · Washington Post · Roll Call

Center-right view

Speaker Mike Johnson and Appropriations Chair Tom Cole call the bill a short-term patch that keeps agencies open at current levels through the election. POLITICO and the Daily Caller report that some House Republicans voted no over the hemp delay, the absence of added immigration funding, and the lack of spending cuts in a Senate draft the president had endorsed.

Sources: POLITICO · Rep. Tom Cole via Government Executive · Daily Caller

Venezuelan oil infrastructure · file

Venezuela

U.S. and Venezuela announce oil agreement covering 65 billion barrels

President Trump said Friday the United States had obtained majority control of that volume of proven reserves through a private operator, North American Blue Energy Partners, and interim President Delcy Rodríguez. A White House fact sheet lists a 35 percent equity stake for the Pentagon’s Office of Strategic Capital and at-cost offtake rights. Rodríguez’s government said the pact covers 17 fields. Chevron is in separate talks to expand its Venezuelan operations.

Center-left view

The New York Times and NPR describe an unusual U.S. government equity position in another country’s fields and a 100-year concession. NPR’s account says the deal will not, by itself, cut U.S. gasoline prices in the near term. Democracy Now! and some energy reporters ask whether the structure is reconstruction finance or a claim on Venezuelan reserves after years of U.S. pressure on Caracas.

Sources: New York Times · Democracy Now! · NPR

Center-right view

The White House fact sheet says there is no cost to U.S. taxpayers, that a majority of the operator’s board must be U.S. citizens, that the contract sits under U.S. law, and that offtake can supply the Strategic Petroleum Reserve and the military from the Western Hemisphere. Secretary of State Marco Rubio, quoted by AP and a Friday pool report, called the agreement a gain for investment in Venezuela and for U.S. reserve depth while Gulf shipping remains disrupted.

Sources: White House fact sheet · Secretary Rubio, Friday pool · Associated Press

Bright Angel corridor after Saturday’s debris flow · NPS / pool

Arizona

Two dead, one missing after flash flood in Grand Canyon National Park

Park officials said rain on the North Rim on Saturday sent water, mud, and rock down Bright Angel Creek. Bridges and a park water line were damaged. Rangers and helicopters brought 82 people out of the Phantom Ranch area. Two bodies were recovered. One person remained unaccounted for on Tuesday. A Border Patrol technical team and search dogs were due on the river. More storms were in the forecast.

Center-left view

National desks and the BBC place the flood in a summer of sudden Western runoff. They note that a creek gauge triggered an alert and that the volume still wrecked a historic inner-canyon corridor. Climate desks in that coverage point to warmer air holding more moisture; they treat monsoon bursts as familiar and the damage scale as the news.

Sources: Tuesday national desk · Morning-show report · BBC News

Center-right view

The Arizona Republic, AP, and KSL quote the National Park Service on a permit roster, a working gauge, and a weekend airlift that accounted for all but one known person in the zone. County and park briefings describe a monsoon debris flow in a slot canyon — a known seasonal hazard — and a search now limited to the river.

Sources: Arizona Republic / NPS briefing · Associated Press · KSL / park update

Gulf Coast

Tropical Storm Edouard makes landfall near Johnson Bayou, Louisiana

The National Hurricane Center placed landfall at 2:20 p.m. CDT with maximum sustained winds of 60 mph. A flood watch covers southeast Texas, including Houston, through Wednesday evening. The Houston National Weather Service said 3–7 inches of rain are expected in parts of eastern Texas, with isolated higher totals where storms train. Utilities reported a few thousand outages. The storm was forecast to weaken inland.

Center-left view

The Weather Channel, El País, and the National Hurricane Center note that warm Gulf water helped a depression become a named storm within a day. That coverage centers on inland flood risk from the Upper Texas coast into east-central Texas, not on wind damage at the coast.

Sources: Weather Channel / NWS Houston · El País · National Hurricane Center

Center-right view

Reuters, the Houston Chronicle, and the USA Today Network report a 60-mph landfall, a 1–2 foot water-level rise in southwestern Louisiana, and energy operators who had already secured plants. Those desks treat Edouard as a short-fuse rainstorm under National Hurricane Center guidance.

Sources: Reuters · Houston Chronicle · USA Today Network
