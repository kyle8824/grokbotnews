Saudi East-West outage threatens up to 4% of global oil supply; Iraq seizes launch platform

UPDATED · Sun. 9:15 a.m. EDT

Saudi oil buyers and traders told Reuters on Sunday that if Riyadh does not restart the East-West (Petroline) pipeline to the Red Sea within days, Yanbu export stocks will run out and up to about 4% of global supply — roughly 4 million barrels a day previously rerouted around Hormuz — could be lost. The line has been shut since Thursday’s drones from Iraq hit near Riyadh and Medina. Iraq’s Security Media Cell said Saturday that forces seized a drone-launch platform used in the attack inside Iraqi territory; Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi had already dismissed Maysan command figures and closed Iran border crossings after launches were tied to that province. In Dublin on Saturday, President Trump said Iran was “probably” responsible and that he had spoken with Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman.

Center-left view

Reuters, CNN, and Al Jazeera frame Sunday’s supply math and Saturday’s Dublin attribution: traders say Yanbu stocks last only days without Petroline, risking ~4 million bpd / ~4% of world supply while Hormuz already constrained; Trump said Iran was “probably” behind the Iraq-origin drones without presenting public evidence; Baghdad dismissed Maysan commanders, closed Iran crossings, and seized a launch platform as Saudi Arabia holds off retaliation to give Iraq investigative space.

Sources: Reuters · CNN · Al Jazeera

Center-right view

Anadolu Agency, Arab News, and Al Arabiya English stress Iraq’s seizure of a launch platform inside its territory, al-Zaidi’s investigation after Maysan-origin drones, and Trump’s Dublin line that Iran is “probably” responsible plus his call with MBS — as Riyadh’s Hormuz-bypass lifeline to Yanbu sits offline and Sunday trader warnings put roughly 4% of global supply at risk if stocks run dry.

Sources: Anadolu Agency · Arab News · Al Arabiya English
