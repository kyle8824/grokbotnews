Trump says Iran ‘probably’ behind Saudi East-West pipeline attack; Iraq confirms drones from its territory

UPDATED · Sat. 4:15 p.m. EDT

In Dublin on Saturday, President Trump said Iran is “probably” responsible for Thursday’s aerial attack on Saudi Arabia’s East-West (Petroline) pipeline — “I think they are, probably they are” — and said he had spoken with Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, calling him a “good friend.” He also said Houthis contacted his administration and prefer the U.S. not become directly involved while “letting most ships go through.” Iraq confirmed the drones launched from its territory; Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi ordered an investigation into Maysan operations command, dismissed the provincial commander (and later the police chief), and closed Iran border crossings after launch sites were reported near the frontier. An Iran-backed militia umbrella denied involvement while praising Houthis. Saudi Arabia shut the pipeline as a precaution and said it would not retaliate for now to give Baghdad room. The line has carried roughly 5 million barrels a day of crude diverted to Yanbu since Hormuz tightened.

Center-left view

CNN, ABC/AP, and Al Jazeera lead with Saturday’s Dublin attribution and Baghdad’s containment push: Trump said Iran was “probably” behind the strike without presenting public evidence; Iraq confirmed launches from Maysan, dismissed the provincial commander and police chief, closed Iran crossings after reported launcher finds, and an Iran-backed Islamic Resistance in Iraq umbrella denied responsibility while praising Houthis. Saudi Energy and Foreign Ministries said the pipeline was shut as a precaution after drones from Iraq hit near Riyadh and Medina, with Riyadh holding off retaliation to give Iraq investigative space as Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb pressure already squeeze exports.

Sources: CNN · ABC / AP · Al Jazeera

Center-right view

Reuters via The Straits Times, Arab News, and Al Arabiya English foreground Trump’s Dublin line that Iran is “probably” responsible, his call with MBS, and Houthi outreach saying they prefer the U.S. stay out while most ships pass — as Riyadh’s Hormuz-bypass lifeline to Yanbu sits offline after Iraq-origin drones and Baghdad races to contain fallout along the Iran border. Reporting pairs the shutdown with Houthi Red Sea advances that already threaten the Bab el-Mandeb leg of Saudi export routes while oil holds above $100 on Friday settles.

Sources: Reuters / Straits Times · Arab News · Al Arabiya English
