Postal Service

Challengers file Supreme Court oppositions to USPS mail-ballot stay as 4 p.m. deadline hits

UPDATED · Wed. 4 p.m. EDT

On the Supreme Court emergency docket in United States Postal Service v. California, No. 26A305, respondents California et al. and the League of Women Voters of Massachusetts et al. filed oppositions Wednesday to the Justice Department’s request to stay U.S. District Judge Indira Talwani’s Sept. 4 preliminary injunction blocking mandatory parts of the Postal Service’s Aug. 26 Ballot Mail for Federal Elections rule for the Nov. 3 midterms. Justice Ketanji Brown Jackson had set a 4 p.m. EDT Sept. 9 response deadline after DOJ withdrew earlier application 26A297 and refiled against the PI. Additional Wednesday filings on the docket include amicus briefs from Harris County, Texas; bipartisan former governors; election officials; and party committees. The Court has not ruled; Talwani’s injunction remains in force unless stayed.

Center-left view

SCOTUSblog coverage of the emergency posture, the Supreme Court docket entries for California’s and LWV Massachusetts’ oppositions, and NBC News reporting on the stay fight frame Wednesday’s filings as states and voting groups urging the Court to leave Talwani’s injunction in place while ballots already mail.

Sources: Supreme Court docket (26A305) · California opposition PDF · NBC News

Center-right view

The DOJ stay application, Washington Examiner, and Fox News stress Solicitor General D. John Sauer’s push to enforce mail-ballot envelope and barcode rules before more states mail, arguing irreparable harm if Talwani’s PI stands through Alabama’s Sept. 9 mailing and later waves.

Sources: DOJ PI stay application · Washington Examiner · Fox News
