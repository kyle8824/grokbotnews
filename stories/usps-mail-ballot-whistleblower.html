Second federal judge blocks USPS mail-ballot rule as SCOTUS still weighs DOJ stay

UPDATED · Mon. ~4:00 p.m. EDT

U.S. District Judge Carl J. Nichols in Washington issued a preliminary injunction late Sunday blocking the Postal Service from enforcing its Aug. 21 Ballot Mail for Federal Elections final rule — including new envelope-design requirements and a federal voter portal — after finding key parts likely exceed Congress’s grant of authority and risk midterm disenfranchisement. The order adds a second nationwide lower-court block alongside Judge Indira Talwani’s earlier Boston injunction, which the First Circuit left in place last week while the Justice Department’s Supreme Court stay application (No. 26A305) remains pending.

Center-left view

Bloomberg Law’s Monday write-through, The Hill’s court-battles report, and UPI’s second-judge coverage cast Nichols’s Sunday PI as another midterm-season setback for Trump’s mail-voting overhaul: a Trump-appointed judge concluding no statute authorizes the portal and voter-list conditions, noting the short window before Nov. 3, and leaving Talwani’s Boston block and the pending SCOTUS stay bid as the administration’s remaining path.

Sources: Bloomberg Law · The Hill · UPI

Center-right view

The Washington Examiner’s Monday Nichols wrap, Just the News’s court brief, and the administration’s still-pending DOJ Supreme Court stay application frame the D.C. injunction as one more lower-court obstacle while Solicitor General Sauer argues the Postal Service’s barcode and recipient-list rules are modest anti-fraud mail regulation — and that irreparable harm follows if injunctions lock in status quo as more states begin mailing ballots.

Sources: Washington Examiner · Just the News · DOJ PI stay application
