Hoskins answers Missouri contempt show-cause, citing federal TRO clash; hearing set Thursday

UPDATED · Wed. 4 p.m. EDT

Secretary of State Denny Hoskins filed his Wednesday response to the Missouri Supreme Court’s show-cause order after he emailed local election officials to use the 2025 HB 1 congressional map under U.S. District Judge Stephen R. Clark’s temporary restraining order. Democracy Docket reported Hoskins also asked the federal district court to shield him from state contempt exposure. In the state filing he argued that unless Clark’s order or a higher court stays it, disregarding the federal TRO would expose him to federal contempt. People Not Politicians’ reply is due 4 p.m. CT Wednesday; Hoskins must appear in person Thursday at 10 a.m. CT. He has not been held in contempt. Justice Brett Kavanaugh’s denial of an emergency stay of the state high court’s 2022-lines/referendum order still stands.

Center-left view

Democracy Docket, Law Dork, and KCTV emphasize the still-binding Missouri Supreme Court injunction, challengers’ claim that a single federal judge cannot overrule the state high court on state law, and Hoskins’s dual track of following Clark’s TRO while seeking federal protection from state contempt.

Sources: Democracy Docket · Law Dork · KCTV

Center-right view

Associated Press reporting via ABC News, NOTUS, and Fox News highlight Hoskins’s argument that Clark’s TRO “halts a full-on constitutional crisis,” the risk of federal contempt if he ignores that order, and GOP efforts to keep the 2025 lines for November amid ballot-print deadlines.
