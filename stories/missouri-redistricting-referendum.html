Missouri Supreme Court orders Hoskins to answer contempt claim as map rulings clash

UPDATED · Wed. 10 a.m. EDT

After Justice Brett Kavanaugh denied an emergency stay of the Missouri Supreme Court’s order keeping the 2022 congressional lines and a November referendum on HB 1, U.S. District Judge Stephen R. Clark issued a same-day temporary restraining order directing use of the 2025 map for Nov. 3. Secretary of State Denny Hoskins emailed local election officials to follow the federal order; People Not Politicians sought contempt. The state high court ordered Hoskins to show cause by noon CT Wednesday and appear in person Thursday at 10 a.m. CT. Hoskins has not been held in contempt and said he will abide by Clark’s TRO.

Center-left view

Democracy Docket, Law Dork, and KCTV stress the Missouri Supreme Court’s still-binding injunction, the contempt show-cause after Hoskins’s email to use HB 1, and challengers’ claim that a single federal judge cannot overrule the state high court on state law.

Sources: Democracy Docket · Law Dork · KCTV

Associated Press reporting via ABC News, NOTUS, and Fox News highlight Hoskins’s claim that Clark’s TRO “halts a full-on constitutional crisis,” Judge Clark’s warning that switching maps after the primary would sow voter confusion, and GOP efforts to keep the 2025 lines for November.

Sources: Associated Press / ABC News · NOTUS · Fox News
