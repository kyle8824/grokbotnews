U.S. Supreme Court blocks use of Missouri’s 2025 GOP congressional map for November

UPDATED · 11:00 a.m. EDT

The U.S. Supreme Court on Thursday granted an emergency request from People Not Politicians and stayed U.S. District Judge Stephen Clark’s temporary restraining order that had required Missouri to use the 2025 HB 1 congressional map in November. NBC News reported the brief order noted no dissents. The stay leaves in force the Missouri Supreme Court’s ruling that the 2022 lines govern the general election and that a referendum on the 2025 map remains on the Nov. 3 ballot. Secretary of State Denny Hoskins and the Trump administration had urged the justices to leave Clark’s order in place, citing primary voters who cast ballots under the new lines and federal ballot deadlines. Hoskins was separately set to appear in person Thursday before the Missouri Supreme Court on a contempt show-cause tied to his earlier emails directing use of the 2025 map.

Center-left view

NBC News, Ballotpedia News, and Democracy Docket frame Thursday’s stay as restoring the Missouri Supreme Court’s 2022-map/referendum order over a single federal TRO, noting no noted SCOTUS dissents and activists’ claim that state constitutional referendum rules blocked the 2025 lines from ever taking effect for November.

Sources: NBC News · Ballotpedia News · Democracy Docket

Center-right view

Fox News and Associated Press coverage, plus Hoskins’s Supreme Court filing summarized by NBC, stress Judge Clark’s concern that switching maps after the primary would disenfranchise voters who already nominated candidates under 2025 lines, federal ballot-mail deadlines, and the Trump Justice Department brief arguing that blocking Clark’s TRO would jeopardize a timely lawful federal election.

Sources: Fox News · Associated Press via Newser · NBC News (Hoskins / DOJ briefs)
