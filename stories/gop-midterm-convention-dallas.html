GOP midterm convention opens in Dallas as Trump flies in for Wednesday keynote

UPDATED · Wed. 4 p.m. EDT

Republicans opened a first-ever national midterm convention Wednesday at the American Airlines Center, with programming afternoon into evening and no nominating business. President Donald Trump left Joint Base Andrews for Dallas after telling reporters some members were skipping because “we don’t have room for 500 politicians,” while an NBC News count found dozens of House Republicans saying they would not attend. Trump is scheduled for Wednesday night’s keynote and returns Thursday after Vice President JD Vance. RNC Chair Joe Gruters cast the gathering as putting “the president on the ballot.” Speakers include Cabinet officials, Texas leaders, and competitive-race candidates; ticket costs for members have been reported near $25,000.

Center-left view

NBC News live updates, The Guardian, and Reuters stress Trump’s low approval, an NBC count of House Republicans skipping Dallas, and the risk of tying midterms to Iran-war and cost-of-living headwinds while the president headlines both nights.

Sources: NBC News live blog · The Guardian · Reuters

Center-right view

Fox News, Washington Examiner, and CBS News Texas highlight Gruters’ turnout pitch that Trump is the best draw for low-propensity voters, host-committee claims of a packed arena, and Trump’s Andrews remarks that members with safe seats should stay home and campaign.

Sources: Fox News · Washington Examiner · CBS News Texas
