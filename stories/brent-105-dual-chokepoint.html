Brent crude tops $105 as Houthis seize Mocha and Hormuz tanker war squeezes shipping

NEW · 7:15 p.m. EDT

Brent crude traded above $105 a barrel Thursday and U.S. crude briefly cleared $100, CNN Business, NBC News, and Reuters reported, as markets priced a dual chokepoint squeeze: Iran-aligned Houthis seized Yemen’s Mocha port and reached the Hanish Islands near Bab el-Mandeb while the U.S.–Iran tanker war kept Hormuz flows far below pre-war levels. Fox News and The National cited government sources saying Saudi-aligned forces pulled toward Dhubab opposite Perim Island; EIA figures cited in that coverage show Bab el-Mandeb volumes rising as Hormuz volumes plunged. Analysts flagged inflation and Fed-rate risks as oil extended gains first set when Brent closed above $100 earlier in the week.

Center-left view

CNN Business, NBC News, and the New York Times frame Thursday’s $105 Brent print and WTI’s brush with $100 as war-risk supply fears spilling into bonds and stocks — diesel and inflation pressure, a more likely Fed hike, and warnings from commodities desks that a prolonged U.S.–Iran stalemate could push Brent toward $120 or higher.

Sources: CNN Business · NBC News · The New York Times

Center-right view

Fox News, Reuters energy desks, and The National stress Iran-backed Houthi seizure of Mocha and the push to Hanish/Bab el-Mandeb as a second chokepoint threat while Hormuz tanker attacks keep Gulf exports constrained — with Brent reported as high as about $106.60 intraday and Saudi Red Sea export routes already under pressure.

Sources: Fox News · Reuters · The National
