Persian Gulf

U.S. and Iran trade the heaviest fire since July as Hormuz shipping stays contested

UPDATED · Wed. 8:30 a.m. EDT

Overnight into Wednesday, U.S. forces struck Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps air-defense, radar, maritime, and communications sites along Iran’s southern coast, and Iran fired missiles and drones at American facilities in Jordan, Bahrain, Kuwait, and northern Iraq, according to CENTCOM and regional militaries. Iranian officials said a U.S. strike hit a house hosting a wedding. Iran’s Revolutionary Guards said two tankers struck naval mines in the Strait of Hormuz. President Trump said he is not seeking a new agreement with Tehran and warned of harder strikes if Iran retaliates. Jordan and Bahrain reported intercepts; the U.S. said it had no casualties from Iranian fire.

A sailor stands watch on a U.S. warship as another vessel crosses the water in the Persian Gulf. File photograph; not from Wednesday’s strikes.

Photo: U.S. Navy via Stars and Stripes

Center-left view

NPR, the Associated Press, and TIME lead with the biggest exchange since July, Iranian reports of civilian deaths at a wedding in Hormozgan, and oil near multi-week highs. Al-Monitor and a Reuters pickup say White House aides want to keep the war relatively quiet until the November midterms while voters already oppose the conflict by wide margins.

Sources: NPR · Associated Press · TIME · Al-Monitor

Center-right view

Fox News Digital, the Washington Examiner, and CNBC’s CENTCOM coverage frame the wave as retaliation for IRGC mine-laying in Hormuz and missiles fired at a U.S. base in Jordan that were intercepted. Trump called the strikes large and powerful, said Hormuz mines had been cleared or detonated, and said he is not seeking a deal while claiming near-total control of the strait.

Sources: Fox News live · Washington Examiner · CNBC / CENTCOM
